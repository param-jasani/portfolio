Local 20-Year-Old Builds a Cluster, Writes the Attacks, Then Catches Them

Param Jasani, blue team engineer and backend developer, has authored every scenario on the HaxNation SOC Simulator and runs a 19-node Kubernetes homelab on the side.

By Our Security Correspondent · Filed from Vasai

MUMBAI — In a nondescript homelab, 19 machines hum in unison: an HAProxy load balancer in front of three control planes, fifteen workers, and 7.5 terabytes of Rook Ceph storage. The design grew from a single control plane to a highly available setup, and storage moved from Longhorn to Ceph to keep up with transformer training. Every decision is documented, in public.

Jasani ranks in the top 1% of TryHackMe's global leaderboard and works the defensive side, having built a Splunk SOC fed by Sysmon from six Windows endpoints, attacked it with MITRE Caldera to prove the detections worked, and wired an IDS-to-SIEM pipeline through the ELK Stack.

"Every scenario is rebuilt from a real campaign: APT29, Kimsuky, RomCom, Emotet."

At HaxNation Mumbai, Jasani is the author of all fourteen scenarios on the SOC Simulator, 321 alerts in total, as well as four Web3 smart-contract challenges and more than ten articles for the community blog.

Before security came backend code at Hellbent Software: REST APIs in Django, FastAPI and Flask, plus Oracle tuning. That background shapes the approach to detection: engineering, with pipelines. Jasani is currently reading for a B.E. in Computer Engineering at VCET, Vasai.
